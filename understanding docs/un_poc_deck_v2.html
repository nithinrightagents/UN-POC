UN EKAP Modernization:
The Portal First, Then the Intelligence

A two-phase programme for modernizing the UN e-Government assessment process. Phase 1 delivers the working assessment portal — questionnaire management, blind double-entry, automatic discrepancy detection, senior arbitration and direct publishing — running entirely on deterministic logic with no AI in the loop. Phase 2 sets out proposed further steps for layering AI on top of that proven portal, headlined by AI pre-fill of the questionnaire so assessors review evidence instead of hunting for it.

Scope & Boundaries 02 / 14

The Online Service Index is one third of the global e-Government Index

OSI · 1/3

Online Service Index (Our Focus) — Evaluated directly by UN DESA. Assesses 102 indicators across national government portals, grouped into six modules: Institutional Framework, Service Provision, Content Provision, Technology, E-Participation and E-Government Literacy.

TII · 1/3

Telecommunication Infrastructure Index (External) — Measures internet access, mobile network coverage and broadband affordability. Data comes directly from the International Telecommunication Union (ITU).

HCI · 1/3

Human Capital Index (External) — Evaluates adult literacy rates, school enrollment and education levels. Sourced directly from UNESCO and UN statistical databases.

Project Focus: This platform covers the Online Service Index (102 indicators) and its city counterpart LOSI (95 indicators). Government questionnaires (MSQs) supply official links and reference material; final scores remain entirely determined by evaluating live public portals.

Both toolkits are already loaded: un_osi_2024_master (102 indicators) and un_losi_2024_master (95 indicators), each carrying the full What / Why / How guidance text per indicator across the same six-module structure.

Phase 1 · The Portal
Phase 1 · Built 04 / 14
Phase 1 — Scope Boundary

The assessment portal, running end-to-end with zero AI in the loop

Phase 1 is strictly the portal: the system of record, and the human workflow that runs on top of it. Every behaviour is deterministic and auditable — no model calls, no probabilistic output, nothing that has to be second-guessed before it can be trusted. That boundary is deliberate. The UN gets a complete, defensible assessment platform that stands on its own, and any AI proposed in Phase 2 would be added to a workflow that is already proven rather than being load-bearing from day one.

In Scope — Phase 1

  • Questionnaire management: master OSI/LOSI templates, custom indicator sets, add / edit / retire / reactivate, and PDF indicator ingestion into an approval queue.
  • Project & unit setup: create a cycle, tag all 193 member states or a custom city group, set per-unit portal URLs, attach MSQ documents.
  • Assessor assignment & project lock: assigning A and B freezes the unit list and the indicator set server-side.
  • Blind double-entry, discrepancy detection, reconciliation, senior arbitration, publishing and the public scorecard.

Explicitly Out of Scope — Phase 1

  • No AI-suggested answers. Assessors answer every indicator themselves.
  • No automated web research or government-portal crawling.
  • No MSQ content mining. MSQ PDFs are attached and readable, not parsed for evidence.
  • No model output of any kind anywhere in the scoring path.

One deterministic exception worth naming: the admin PDF indicator ingestion is a rule-based text parser, not a model. Every extracted candidate is staged as a pending indicator awaiting explicit admin approval — nothing reaches a live questionnaire without a human clicking Approve.

Phase 1 · Operational Lifecycle
Phase 1 · Built 05 / 14

The 8-Stage Assessment & Publishing Lifecycle — every stage human-driven

Stage 01 · Admin Project Setup Create a cycle, choose National OSI (102) or City LOSI (95), and tag the units to be assessed
Stage 02 · Admin Questionnaire Tailoring Add, edit, retire or reactivate indicators — branching a custom set and leaving the master template untouched
Stage 03 · Gate Assign & Lock Assigning Assessor A and B freezes the unit list and indicator set so the discrepancy maths cannot shift mid-cycle
Stage 04 · Assessors Blind Double-Entry A and B answer independently — answer, evidence URL and notes — with neither able to read the other
Stage 05 · Gate Discrepancy Detection Once both roles declare complete, the system compares them and opens a round only if divergence exceeds project tolerance (default 5%)
Stage 06 · Assessors Reconciliation Round Contested indicators only, shown side by side and no longer blind, each settled with a written joint justification
Stage 07 · Reviewer Senior Arbitration Anything unresolved after one round escalates: the reviewer reads both entries, then returns it for another round or decides each indicator
Stage 08 · Public Publish The approved record becomes the public scorecard directly — no CSV export, no re-upload, no reformatting step
Answer precedence at publish time: a senior reviewer's arbitrated answer takes priority, then the assessors' joint reconciliation answer, then a straight A/B consensus. Every published score traces back to a named human decision.

One-round rule: a reconciliation round runs once. If assessors still disagree it is force-closed as exhausted and raised to the Senior Reviewer — no indefinite ping-pong between assessors.

Phase 1 · Integrity Controls
Phase 1 · Built 06 / 14

What Keeps the Numbers Honest: four enforced guarantees

01. Lock

Assignment Freezes the Comparison Basis — the moment Assessor A and B are assigned, the project locks. Adding, removing, retiring or bulk-editing units and indicators is refused at the route level, not merely hidden in the UI. The lock covers exactly the two things that would silently corrupt the discrepancy calculation if changed mid-assessment.

02. Blindness

Role-Scoped Reads — Assessor A cannot retrieve Assessor B's answers, evidence or notes while assessment is live. Blindness lifts only at reconciliation, and only for the indicators actually in dispute.

03. Tolerance

Configurable Threshold, Both-Complete Trigger — the divergence tolerance is per-project (default 5%) and remains editable while locked. A round opens only once both roles have declared complete, so a half-finished assessor cannot trip a false alarm.

04. Audit

Every Decision Attributed — each answer, evidence link, joint settlement, arbitration disposition and publication is recorded against the actor who made it. The public scorecard reads from the same record the reviewer approved, so there is no untracked gap between decision and publication.

Deliberately still editable while locked: per-unit MSQ upload, project tolerance, the publish action, the discrepancy and arbitration queue, and project deletion. Locking is targeted, not a blanket freeze — and an Unassign & Reopen escape hatch exists for a mistyped assessor email.

Escalation is bounded by design: the reviewer's two options are return for another reconciliation round (which requires a written reason) or decide each disputed indicator directly (which requires a Yes/No on every one, enforced server-side).

Phase 2 · Where AI Comes In
Phase 2 · Proposed 08 / 14
Phase 2 — Scope Boundary

AI is added around the portal, never inside the decision

Phase 2 is a set of proposed further steps. It would introduce AI at four points in and around the lifecycle — and at none of them would a model decide a score. The portal's stages, gates and audit trail from Phase 1 would stay exactly as they are; AI simply arrives earlier in the process with evidence already gathered, so the human work shifts from searching to verifying. If none of this is taken up, or the AI layer is switched off later, Phase 1 keeps running unchanged.

01
AI Pre-Fill of the Questionnaire The headline proposal. Would draft an answer plus cited evidence for each indicator, before the assessor opens the form
02
MSQ Document Mining Would read 50-plus page government self-reports and surface the official links and claims into a per-unit reference panel
03
Accuracy Calibration Harness Would score AI output against published UN 2024 results so precision is measured rather than asserted
04
In-Portal AI Assistant Would answer plain-language questions about a cycle by retrieving the portal's own records on its own initiative — read-only, always sourced
The governing principle for anything in this phase: AI output would enter the portal as a draft attached to an indicator, never as a submitted answer. Blind double-entry, discrepancy detection, reconciliation, arbitration and publishing stay untouched — an assessor would still have to accept, edit or reject every single suggestion.

Why this ordering: the failure mode in earlier AI trials was not a weak model but the absence of a workflow to catch it. Phase 1 provides that workflow first, so any suggestion proposed here would land in a system already designed to disagree with it.

Phase 2 · Headline Proposal
Phase 2 · Proposed 09 / 14

AI Pre-Fill for the Questionnaire: the assessor reviews instead of researches

Today an assessor opens a blank form and works through 102 (or 95) indicators for a single country or city, finding and reading the relevant government pages for each one. Pre-fill would invert that: before the assessor ever opens the unit, the system would already have proposed an answer for each indicator and attached the evidence it relied on. The assessor's job becomes confirming or correcting a cited draft — the same decision, reached far faster, with the reasoning visible. The steps below are the proposed shape of that pipeline.

Step 01 Resolve the Target Links Three tiers in order: previously verified links for that unit, then official links declared by the government in its MSQ, then public web search as a last resort
Step 02 Search the Way a Citizen Would Deliberately shallow navigation across top-level pages and main menus. A service a real citizen cannot find should not score as if it were available
Step 03 Draft Against the Indicator's Own Guidance Each proposal is generated against that indicator's What / Why / How text, so the model answers the question the toolkit actually asks
Step 04 Cross-Check With a Second Model Two independent models review each indicator separately — one on literal text, one on context — with no shared reasoning to correlate their mistakes
Step 05 Verify Every Citation Live Each quoted snippet and link is re-tested against the real page. A citation that does not resolve invalidates the draft rather than decorating it
Step 06 Gate, Then Hand Over Only drafts where both models agree and confidence clears the threshold surface as suggestions. Everything else, including anything behind a login, goes to the assessor unfilled and flagged
What the assessor sees: a proposed answer, the exact quoted passage supporting it, a live link, the confidence figure, and whether both models agreed. Three actions are always available — accept, edit, or override completely — and every one of them is recorded against the assessor, not the model.

Blindness would be preserved: both assessors receive the same pre-fill drafts independently and still cannot see each other's submissions. Because A and B may accept or reject the same suggestion differently, discrepancy detection remains a genuine cross-check rather than two copies of one model's opinion.

Phase 2 · Reliability Engineering
Phase 2 · Proposed 10 / 14

Making Pre-Fill Trustworthy: proposed answers to the known failure modes

Known Failure Mode Why It Happened Before Proposed Countermeasure
Fabricated evidence Model quoted text or links that were not on the page Live citation verification: every snippet and URL is re-fetched and matched against the actual page before the draft is shown
Confident but wrong A single model with no second opinion, right roughly 80% of the time Two independent models reviewing separately; disagreement suppresses the suggestion instead of averaging it away
Login walls Crawler stalled or guessed at authenticated services Explicit escalation: anything behind authentication is routed to the assessor unfilled and clearly marked, never inferred
Over-deep crawling Buried pages found by machine that no citizen would ever reach Shallow navigation limits mirroring realistic citizen behaviour, so accessibility is scored as experienced, not as indexed
Non-English portals Coverage thinned sharply outside English-language sites Multilingual evaluation across the major portal languages, with the original-language quote retained alongside the assessment
Unmeasured accuracy Performance claimed anecdotally, never benchmarked Calibration against published UN 2024 results for known units, reported as precision and recall per module
Silent drift No visibility once a run was underway Run telemetry: per-indicator agreement rate, latency and cost surfaced to the admin for every batch
Proposed acceptance test: pre-fill would only be turned on for real assessment once measured accuracy on benchmark units clears an agreed bar and the assessor override rate is visible. Until that point drafts can be generated for evaluation, while the portal continues to operate exactly as it does in Phase 1.

Failure would be quiet by design: if the AI layer is unavailable, times out, or returns nothing for a unit, the assessor simply sees the ordinary blank Phase 1 form. No stage of the lifecycle would depend on a model being reachable.

Phase 2 · Supporting Proposals
Phase 2 · Proposed 11 / 14

Around the Pre-Fill: two supporting proposals

MSQ Document Mining

  • The problem: each government submits a 50-plus page self-report questionnaire full of official service URLs. Today it reaches the assessor as a raw PDF with no index, so genuine evidence is routinely missed simply because nobody read page 38.
  • What is proposed: the MSQ would be parsed on upload, surfacing the official links and declared claims into a per-unit reference panel alongside the indicator being assessed.
  • How it would feed pre-fill: MSQ-declared links would become tier two of the link resolution chain — trusted above open web search, because the government itself named them.
  • What it explicitly would not be: a scoring input. A government claiming a service exists never scores the indicator. The claim only tells the assessor and the AI where to look; the live portal still decides.

Accuracy Calibration Harness

  • Ground truth: published UN 2024 results for known units provide a scored answer key to measure against.
  • What is measured: precision and recall per module, agreement rate between the two models, and how often an assessor overrides an accepted suggestion.
  • Archetype coverage: deliberately diverse portals — differing languages, platform maturity and site structures — so accuracy is not tuned to one style of government website.
  • Why it comes first: the pre-fill quality bar would be set from this evidence rather than chosen arbitrarily, and re-run whenever a model or prompt changes.
Everything AI-generated would stay visibly labelled. A pre-filled answer an assessor accepted would be recorded as accepted-from-suggestion, distinctly from one they authored — so UN DESA could report exactly how much of a published cycle originated as an AI draft, a question a reviewer or auditor is entitled to ask.

Hosting posture would be unchanged by Phase 2: the AI layer runs against configurable model endpoints on UN-controlled infrastructure. No assessment data would leave the deployment boundary as a condition of using it.

Phase 2 · Proposal · AI Portal Assistant
Phase 2 · Proposed 12 / 14

An AI Assistant Inside the Portal: ask in plain language, it retrieves on its own

Everything the portal knows — every cycle, unit, indicator, answer, evidence link, disagreement, arbitration note and publication — already sits in one database. Today, getting an answer out of it means knowing which screen to open. The proposed assistant closes that gap: a chat panel in the portal where a user asks a question in ordinary language, and the assistant works out for itself which records to fetch, retrieves them, and answers with the sources attached. No report has to be built in advance for a question to be answerable.

Assessor asks Which indicators have I not answered yet for Kenya, and which ones did we mark unavailable last cycle? Retrieves from this assessor's own submissions plus the previous cycle's published record — never the other assessor's live answers
Senior Reviewer asks Show me every unit still above tolerance, and for Peru what exactly did A and B disagree on? Retrieves from the discrepancy and arbitration queue, returning the per-indicator A/B comparison with both sets of evidence and notes
Admin asks Which projects are locked and awaiting assessment, and how many indicators changed between the two toolkits? Retrieves from project status and questionnaire versions, diffing the active indicator sets rather than reading a prepared report
Analyst asks How has Denmark moved across cycles, and which module drove the change? Retrieves from published scorecards only, breaking the movement down by module across cycles

How It Retrieves Autonomously

  • It plans its own lookups. From the question, the assistant decides which records it needs — project, unit, indicator, cycle, actor, date range — and fetches them itself, rather than being limited to a fixed set of canned reports.
  • It reads the portal's own material. Assessment records, indicator What / Why / How guidance, attached MSQ documents and published scorecards are all in scope for retrieval.
  • It answers with sources. Every statement carries the record it came from — project, unit, indicator, who entered it and when — with a link straight to that screen in the portal.
  • It says when it cannot find something rather than filling the gap, because an unfounded answer about a live assessment is worse than no answer.

Guardrails

  • Read-only. The assistant retrieves and explains. It never submits an answer, closes a reconciliation round, arbitrates a dispute or publishes a score.
  • Blindness enforced at the query layer. It inherits the caller's permissions from the portal, so an assessor's assistant cannot surface the other assessor's live answers — the same rule the portal enforces, applied to retrieval.
  • Nothing unsourced. An answer that cannot be tied to a portal record is not returned as fact.
  • Fully logged. What was asked, what was retrieved and what was shown is recorded, so assistant usage is as auditable as any other portal action.

Why it is worth proposing: the portal already holds the answers to most operational questions asked during a cycle — progress, disagreement, precedent, history. The assistant removes the navigation cost of getting to them, without adding a new place where data lives or a new way for a score to be set.
