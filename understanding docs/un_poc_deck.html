Scope & Boundaries 02 / 12

The Online Service Index is one third of the global e-Government Index

OSI · 1/3

Online Service Index (Our Focus) — Evaluated directly by UN DESA. Assesses 102 core questions across national government portals: public services, government information, digital participation, technical standards, and digital literacy.

TII · 1/3

Telecommunication Infrastructure Index (External) — Measures internet access, mobile network coverage, and broadband affordability. Data comes directly from the International Telecommunication Union (ITU).

HCI · 1/3

Human Capital Index (External) — Evaluates adult literacy rates, school enrollment, and education levels. Sourced directly from UNESCO and UN statistical databases.

Project Focus: This platform focuses exclusively on the Online Service Index (102 questions) and its city counterpart (LOSI — 95 questions). Government questionnaires (MSQs) provide official links and reference material, while final country scores are 100% determined by evaluating live public portals.

City-Level Evaluation (LOSI): Assesses 95 local public services (e.g. municipal permits, local transport info, city taxes, waste management). City rankings depend entirely on the Local Online Service Index produced by this platform.

Deep Dive · Key Challenges 01 & 02 04 / 12

Data Organization & Website Publishing

Key Challenge 01 · Current Baseline Manual Spreadsheets
Everything Lives in Fragile Spreadsheets
Assessments across 193 nations are tracked in separate Google Sheets and folders (e.g., Afghanistan 0-1, Afghanistan 0-2). Staff must run manual steps to compare, clean, and merge files.
  • Risk of Accidental Errors: Vulnerable to accidental cell overwrites, broken formulas, and mixed-up files.
  • Past Data Trapped: 4–5 cycles of past survey data are locked in different spreadsheets, making multi-year comparisons painful.
  • No Role Separation: No built-in way to keep Assessor A and B blind to each other's answers.
The Modern Solution · How It Works Secure Database
Unified Secure Database with Full History Tracking
Replaces scattered spreadsheets with a single, secure central database that manages survey cycles, country/city targets, and assessments while permanently recording all activity.
  • Guaranteed Blind Scoring: Built-in permissions ensure Assessor A and B cannot view each other's work until both finish.
  • Easy Past Data Import: Automated tools import past survey spreadsheets into clean, standardized records.
  • Complete Activity Log: Every answer, supporting link, score change, and supervisor approval is permanently tracked.
Secure Central Database Full Activity History Role-Based Access
Key Challenge 02 · Current Baseline System Silos
Survey System & Public Website Are Disconnected
The internal survey intake system and the public UN Knowledgebase are separate systems that do not talk to each other.
  • Heavy Manual Transfer: Releasing the report requires manually exporting CSV files and re-uploading them.
  • Time-Consuming Web Updates: Country profile pages and rankings must be manually updated and checked by hand.
  • Months of Delay: Long delays between final assessment sign-off and public availability on the UN website.
The Modern Solution · How It Works Direct Publishing
Instant 1-Click Publishing to Public Website
A connected platform where a supervisor's final sign-off instantly updates the public UN Knowledgebase, country profiles, and global rankings.
  • Instant Public Updates: No manual spreadsheet formatting or copy-pasting between assessment completion and publication.
  • Direct Data Feeds: Clean, standard data feeds connect seamlessly to any existing UN website or dashboard.
  • Interactive Public Dashboards: Instant rankings, country profiles, and multi-year comparisons ready on day one.
1-Click Publishing Instant Website Sync Connected Data Feeds
Deep Dive · Key Challenges 03 & 04 05 / 12

Question Updates & Multi-City Studies

Key Challenge 03 · Current Baseline Changing Questions
~50% Question Changes Break Past Comparisons
e-Government evolves fast; about half of the 102 National and 95 City questions change, update, or retire between survey cycles, making long-term tracking difficult.
  • Repetitive Work: Assessors must re-test basic, unchanged government websites from scratch every single cycle.
  • Broken Multi-Year Trends: When questions change wording or format, tracking progress over time becomes difficult.
  • Scattered Guidelines: Question definitions and scoring rules are often spread across separate PDF documents.
The Modern Solution · How It Works Smart Carry-Forward
Smart Question Updates & "Carry-Forward" Past Answers
Clean management of survey questions (active, updated, or retired) combined with an automated feature that brings forward past verified answers for quick confirmation.
  • Carry-Forward Speed: Previously confirmed positive features pre-load automatically for rapid 1-click human verification.
  • Flexible Question Updates: Administrators can add, update, or retire questions per cycle without breaking past records.
  • Clear Multi-Year History: Keeps track of how questions evolved over time for accurate historical trend charts.
Question Lifecycle 1-Click Carry-Forward Historical Tracking
Key Challenge 04 · Current Baseline City Limits
City Assessment Built for 1 City — Demand is for Multi-City
Standard city evaluations cover only the single largest city per country. Member states increasingly request evaluations for multiple cities across their region.
  • Isolated Regional Projects: Custom city requests (e.g. UK 8 cities, Argentina, Greece) have to be built in separate, disconnected sheets.
  • Inconsistent Methods: Ad-hoc city surveys risk drifting away from the core UN 95-indicator Toolkit standard.
  • Heavy Administrative Burden: UN staff spend excessive time managing custom city projects manually.
The Modern Solution · How It Works Multi-City Ready
Flexible Country & Multi-City Project Builder
A versatile system designed to assess both national portals (102 questions) and multiple cities (95 questions) within the exact same platform.
  • Instant Project Creation: Administrators can set up custom regional projects (e.g. 8 UK cities) in seconds with standard templates.
  • Shared Quality Tools: City assessments use the exact same AI research, discrepancy checks, and review tools as national surveys.
  • Unified Knowledgebase: City profiles and national rankings sit together in one organized database.
Multi-City Ready 95-Question City Toolkit Standardized Methods
Deep Dive · Key Challenges 05 & 06 06 / 12

Quality Control & Government Form Extraction

Key Challenge 05 · Current Baseline Manual Review
Disagreements Spotted Manually by Eyeballing Spreadsheets
Supervisors must manually compare Assessor A and B answers line-by-line across all 102 questions for every country. There is no automated warning when scores diverge.
  • Reviewer Fatigue: Supervisors must check every question line-by-line, even when both assessors agreed 100%.
  • No Clear Dispute Process: Lack of a structured comparison screen makes resolving differences slow and subjective.
  • Risk of Missed Conflicts: Large score differences can slip through manual checks without automated warnings.
The Modern Solution · How It Works Automatic Flagging
Automatic Discrepancy Alerts & Side-by-Side Resolution
When two assessors differ by more than 5%, the system automatically flags the unit and brings both assessors into a clear side-by-side comparison screen to agree on the right answer.
  • Side-by-Side Comparison: Assessors clearly see their own answer and link next to their peer's submission for conflicting questions.
  • Focus on Differences Only: Agreed questions remain locked; assessors focus their time only on disputed items.
  • One Agreed Submission: Assessors submit a single unified answer with their reasoning, ready for supervisor sign-off.
5% Disagreement Alert Side-by-Side Screen Consensus Workflow
Key Challenge 06 · Current Baseline Trapped PDFs
50+ Page Government Questionnaires Go Unindexed
Governments submit 50+ page self-reports (MSQs) listing official portals and links. The UN lacks the staff to manually index these, so they are given to assessors as raw PDFs.
  • Missed Official Links: Assessors miss ministry links buried in long PDFs and mistakenly give a score of 0.
  • Wasted Research Time: Assessors spend hours searching the web for links the government already provided.
  • Trapped Information: Useful government sub-portals remain locked inside unstructured PDF files.
The Modern Solution · How It Works Smart PDF Extractor
Automatic Link Extraction from Government Questionnaires
The system automatically scans 50+ page government forms (MSQs) and organizes all official links (e-services, open data, permits, tax) into an easy reference panel for AI and assessors.
  • 3-Step Link Lookup: Checks: (1) Past verified links → (2) Official links submitted by government → (3) Public web search.
  • Reference Only (No Biased Scores): Supplies official URLs for quick checking without letting self-reports automatically award points.
  • Zero Blind Spots: Ensures assessors evaluate the exact official portals provided by national authorities.
Automatic PDF Reader Official Link Panel Unbiased Reference
Deep Dive · Key Challenges 07 & 08 07 / 12

Reliable AI & UN Operational Governance

Key Challenge 07 · Current Baseline Unreliable AI
Past AI Trials: ~80% Accuracy & Deep-Link Errors
Previous AI scraping tests had an ~80% accuracy rate with unpredictable errors. Automated crawlers frequently found obscure, deeply buried links that ordinary citizens cannot realistically find.
  • Diplomatic Risk: Unverified AI errors or false scores on sovereign nations create credibility risks for the UN.
  • Too-Deep Web Scraping: Scrapers finding hidden, buried links violate the rule that services must be easy for citizens to find.
  • Login Barriers: National digital ID login walls (like Turkey e-Devlet) block automated crawlers.
The Modern Solution · How It Works Controlled AI
Double-Checking AI Assistants with Strict Citizen Rules
AI operates strictly as a research assistant with citizen-level search limits. Two separate AI models verify every finding, and human experts make 100% of final scoring decisions.
  • Citizen-Level Navigation: AI searches only easy-to-find public pages — if a citizen cannot find a service within a few clicks, it cannot score positive.
  • Two AI Assistants Cross-Check: Two separate AI models independently verify each question to eliminate errors.
  • Strict 75%+ Confidence Gate: Any uncertain finding (<75%), conflicting evidence, or login-gated portal immediately escalates to human assessors.
Citizen Findability Dual AI Cross-Check 75% Confidence Filter

Operational Governance Flow 08 / 12

The 8-Stage Assessment & Publishing Lifecycle

Stage 01 · Admin Project Setup Set up survey year, choose National (102 items) or City (95 items), and assign assessor teams
Stage 02 · System Government Form Reading Automatically extract official links from 50+ page government forms into a reference panel
Stage 03 · AI Engine Double-Check AI Search Two AI assistants research portals using citizen search rules; flag verified items with ≥75% confidence
Stage 04 · Assessors Blind Double-Entry Assessor A & Assessor B evaluate independently without seeing each other's answers
Stage 05 · Gate Discrepancy Check System automatically flags any country or city where assessors differ by more than 5%
Stage 06 · Assessors Side-by-Side Resolution Assessors compare differing answers on a split screen, agree, and submit 1 final answer
Stage 07 · Reviewer Senior Sign-Off Senior UN supervisor reviews evidence, resolves remaining questions, and approves scores
Stage 08 · Public Instant Web Publish Approved scores publish instantly to the UN Knowledgebase with live rankings and profiles
Strict Governance Guarantee: Human experts are always in control. AI acts only as a research assistant; every published score is backed by verified human approval and a permanent audit trail.

Discrepancy Threshold: Configurable per project (default: 5% differing answers). Units where assessors agree 100% pass smoothly to senior review.

AI Assistant Architecture 09 / 12

Inside the AI Assistant & Verification Engine

01. Find Links

3-Tier Link Lookup — Gathers target website links in order: (1) Past verified links → (2) Official links submitted by governments in MSQs → (3) Public web search.

02. Search

Search Like a Citizen (Shallow Navigation) — Searches only top-level pages and main menus — strictly avoiding deep hidden links so ratings reflect true public accessibility.

03. Double-Check

Two AI Assistants Review Independently — Two distinct AI models review each question separately (one checks exact text, one checks context) with no shared bias.

04. Validate

Live Citation Verification — A dedicated verification check tests every extracted text snippet and link against the real live webpage to prevent hallucinations.

05. Escalate

High-Confidence Filter (≥75%) & Human Escalation — Only suggestions with ≥75% confidence and AI agreement are shown as drafts; anything uncertain or behind logins is sent directly to human experts.

Safety Guarantee: AI suggestions are never published automatically. Assessors can accept, modify, or completely override suggestions with 1-click simplicity.
