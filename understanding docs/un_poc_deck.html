Index Architecture & Scoping Boundary 02 / 12

The Online Service Index is one third of the global EGDI

OSI · 1/3

Online Service Index (In Scope) — Directly evaluated by UN DESA. Assesses 102 binary indicators from the National e-Government Toolkit across national portals: institutional frameworks, service provision, content provision, technology standards, e-participation, and literacy.

TII · 1/3

Telecommunication Infrastructure Index (External) — Measures internet users, mobile broadband/cellular penetration, and service affordability. Data sourced directly from the International Telecommunication Union (ITU).

HCI · 1/3

Human Capital Index (External) — Evaluates adult literacy rates, school enrollment, and education metrics. Sourced directly from UNESCO and secondary UN statistical feeds.

Project Scoping Boundary: This platform is strictly focused on modernizing the OSI (102 indicators) and its municipal counterpart (LOSI — 95 indicators). MSQ government submissions serve strictly as link and metadata reference layers for assessors and AI pre-fill; sovereign scores are 100% determined by primary portal evaluations.

City-Level Evaluation (LOSI): Evaluates 95 binary indicators from the Local e-Government Toolkit (e.g., parking ticket payments, tourist services, building permits). Disaggregated TII and HCI data does not exist globally; city rankings depend 100% on the Local Online Service Index produced by this platform.

Deep Dive · Key Challenges 01 & 02 04 / 12

Data Fragmentation & Publishing Disconnect

Key Challenge 01 · Current Baseline Manual Process
Everything Lives in Fragile Spreadsheets
Assessments across 193 nations are tracked in separate Google Sheets and folders (e.g., Afghanistan 0-1, Afghanistan 0-2). Evaluation staff must run ad-hoc scripts to compare and merge submissions.
  • Zero Data Integrity: Vulnerable to accidental cell overwrites, schema mismatches, and broken formulas.
  • 4–5 Cycles Trapped: Historical data across prior editions lacks a standardized structure, preventing longitudinal analysis.
  • No Role Boundaries: No enforced permissions between blind assessors, reviewers, and admins.
Engineered Resolution · Implemented Relational Core
Unified Database & Workflow State Machine
Replaces disparate spreadsheets with a centralized, ACID-compliant database (SQLite / PostgreSQL) managing Cycles, Projects, Units, and Assessments with an append-only audit trail.
  • Strict Blindness Enforcement: Query-level data isolation guarantees Assessor A and B cannot see each other's work.
  • Automated Legacy Ingestion: Migration pipelines convert 4–5 cycles of historical survey sheets into structured records.
  • Audit Traceability: Every answer submission, confidence score, and reviewer override is permanently logged.
SQLite / PostgreSQL Append-Only Schema State Machine
Key Challenge 02 · Current Baseline System Silos
Frontend & Backend Completely Disconnected
The assessment intake backend (.NET Nuke / eGov Survey) and the public-facing E-Government Knowledgebase (EGKB) are separate silos that do not communicate.
  • Manual Transfer Overhead: Releasing a biennial report requires manually exporting CSVs and re-importing into the CMS.
  • Static Page Maintenance: Public country pages and rankings must be manually updated and hand-coded.
  • Release Bottleneck: Months of delay between final assessment sign-off and public data availability.
Engineered Resolution · Implemented Direct Pipeline
Single-Source Direct Publishing & REST API
Unified platform where Senior Reviewer approval automatically projects verified scores to the public knowledge base, instantly updating country profiles, rankings, and exports.
  • Instant Knowledge Base Sync: Zero manual data wrangling or spreadsheet re-formatting between assessment and release.
  • Headless Programmatic API: /api/v1 REST API exposes clean JSON endpoints for cycles, units, and scores.
  • Public Analytics Engine: Dynamic leaderboards, regional filters, and time-series comparisons out of the box.
FastAPI Headless /api/v1 Instant Projection
Deep Dive · Key Challenges 03 & 04 05 / 12

Indicator Churn & Municipal LOSI Scaling

Key Challenge 03 · Current Baseline Schema Drift
~50% Indicator Churn Breaks Longitudinal Mapping
Digital government evolves rapidly; ~50% of the 102 National and 95 LOSI indicators change, retire, or are introduced across editions. Mapping multi-cycle data is complex and prone to schema breakage.
  • Redundant Manual Work: Assessors must re-evaluate unchanged baseline features from scratch every single cycle.
  • Broken Trend Analytics: Changing question formulations make multi-year progress tracking inaccurate.
  • No Version Control: Indicator definitions and scoring guidelines are scattered across ad-hoc PDFs.
Engineered Resolution · Implemented Lifecycle Engine
Versioned Indicators & "Carry-Forward" Engine
First-class indicator lifecycle management (active, modified, retired, new) paired with an automated baseline carry-forward acceleration engine across both national and municipal toolkits.
  • Carry-Forward Acceleration: Prior positive assessments pre-populate as verified baselines for rapid 1-click human confirmation.
  • Dynamic Admin Governance: Admins can add, retire, or modify indicators per cycle without altering past survey schemas.
  • Longitudinal Lineage: Preserves historical indicator mappings for reliable multi-cycle trend visualizations.
Schema Versioning Carry-Forward Logic Historical Mapping
Key Challenge 04 · Current Baseline Architecture Bottleneck
LOSI Built for 1 City — Demand is for Multi-City
Standard LOSI evaluates only the most populous city per nation across 95 Local Toolkit indicators. Member states increasingly demand assessments for multiple cities across national jurisdictions.
  • One-Off Siloed Implementations: Each regional request (e.g. UK 8 cities, Argentina, Greece) must be built as a standalone project with disconnected sheets.
  • Inconsistent Methodologies: Ad-hoc city surveys drift from the core UN 95-indicator Toolkit standard.
  • Administrative Overhead: Secretariat staff cannot scale custom regional projects without significant manual effort.
Engineered Resolution · Implemented Polymorphic Units
Polymorphic "Unit of Study" & Project Spawner
Flexible domain model abstracting evaluation targets into "Units of Study" (National Portals, Capital Cities, Regional Municipalities) running on a single unified platform.
  • Self-Service Project Templates: Admins instantiate custom initiatives (e.g. LOSI UK with 8 cities) in seconds with the modular 95-indicator municipal package.
  • Shared Assessment Mechanics: Custom municipal projects reuse the exact same AI pre-fill, discrepancy, and review pipelines.
  • Unified Knowledgebase: City profiles (95 indicators) and national rankings (102 indicators) sit side-by-side in the same database.

Deep Dive · Key Challenges 05 & 06 06 / 12

Discrepancy Review & MSQ Context Resolution

Key Challenge 05 · Current Baseline Subjective Review
Discrepancies Resolved Manually by Eyeballing
Reviewers must manually eyeball Assessor A vs. B answers line-by-line across 102 National / 95 LOSI questions across all units. No automated mathematical threshold or structured escalation exists.
  • Reviewer Fatigue: Staff must inspect all units equally, even when assessors achieved 100% consensus.
  • Arbitration Gaps: Lack of formal reconciliation protocols leads to subjective score settling.
  • No Discrepancy Alerting: Wide assessor divergence can slip through manual checks without system warnings.
Engineered Resolution · Implemented In-Platform Reconciliation
Automated Discrepancy Engine & Dual-Context Joint Reconciliation
When divergence exceeds the 5% threshold, surveys return to both assessors in a comparative reconciliation workspace, showing each what they and their peer entered to submit a unified answer.
  • Reciprocal Side-by-Side Diff: Assessors see their own initial answer/URL side-by-side with their peer's submission for conflicting indicators.
  • Isolated Conflict View: Consensual indicators remain locked; assessors focus exclusively on negotiating and resolving disputed features.
  • Unified Joint Submission: Assessors agree on a single final score with logged justification, automatically routing clean data to Senior Reviewer sign-off.
5% Threshold Alert Reciprocal Diff View Unified Consensus Flow
Key Challenge 06 · Current Baseline Information Silo
50+ Page MSQ Submissions Go Unindexed
Member states submit 50+ page Member State Questionnaires (MSQs) declaring official sub-portals and ministerial links. Secretariat lacks resources to parse these, handing them to assessors as raw PDFs.
  • Overlooked Government URLs: Assessors miss official ministerial links buried in 50-page PDFs and falsely score 0.
  • Search Inefficiencies: Assessors spend hours searching search engines for links the government already declared.
  • Link Siloing: Rich sub-portal directories declared by authorities remain locked inside unstructured PDFs.
Engineered Resolution · Implemented Link Reference Layer
MSQ Ingestion Engine for Reference & Pre-Fill
Automated ingestion of MSQ submissions extracts declared official sub-portals (e-services, open data, e-participation, procurement) into a structured link reference layer for AI pre-fill and assessors.
  • 3-Tier Link Resolution: Resolves URLs systematically: (1) Historical KB → (2) MSQ Declared Official URLs → (3) Search discovery.
  • Link Reference Only (Zero Score Bias): Provides official URLs directly to AI agents and assessors without letting self-reports directly award scores.
  • Zero Blind Spots: Guarantees evaluation against the exact official sub-portals nominated by national governments.
MSQ Link Parser Link Resolution Reference Layer
Deep Dive · Key Challenges 07 & 08 07 / 12

AI Reliability & Institutional Governance

Key Challenge 07 · Current Baseline Unpredictable AI
Past AI Trials: ~80% Accuracy & Deep-Link Drift
Previous AI scraping trials had an ~80% accuracy rate with unpredictable error drift across countries. Autonomous crawlers frequently scraped deep, hidden sub-links that ordinary citizens cannot realistically navigate to.
  • Diplomatic & Credibility Risk: Unverified AI hallucinations or scoring errors on sovereign nations damage UN authority.
  • Deep Scraping False Positives: Scrapers uncovering obscure, buried URLs violate the core citizen "easy findability" rule.
  • Login Barriers: National portals behind eID systems (e.g. Turkey e-Devlet) block automated crawlers.
Engineered Resolution · Implemented Dual-Agent HITL
Dual-Agent Pre-Fill & Shallow Traversal Rule
AI functions strictly as an evidence pre-fill accelerator with shallow traversal limits that simulate citizen findability. Humans retain 100% scoring authority.
  • Shallow Citizen Traversal: Crawler avoids deep link exploration, scoring 0 if features are not accessible within standard citizen navigation paths.
  • Dual-Agent Verification: Two independent Gemini models (Literal Reader vs Inferential Evaluator) must cross-verify findings.
  • Strict Confidence Gate (≥75%): Results with <75% confidence, conflicting evidence, or login walls automatically escalate to human assessors.
Shallow Findability Gemini Dual-Agent ≥75% Confidence Gate
Key Challenge 08 · Institutional Constraint Procurement Roadblocks
Institutional Hosting & SaaS Procurement Rules
Institutional restrictions on recurring credit card micro-transactions and rigid cloud procurement workflows prevent adoption of standard commercial SaaS tools. Central vendor procurement cycles are lengthy (12–24+ months).
  • No Monthly SaaS Billing: Systems requiring monthly recurring card charges cannot be adopted by UN DESA.
  • Strict Data Residency: UN sovereign data rules require enterprise-controlled hosting (.NET/Drupal/UN Cloud).
  • Procurement Inertia: Institutional vendor bidding prevents programme leadership from engaging trusted technical partners directly.
Engineered Resolution · Implemented Turnkey Delivery
Self-Contained Stack & Consultant Delivery
Portable, zero-recurring-cost architecture engineered to run on UN enterprise infrastructure or long-term prepaid enterprise cloud instances.
  • Zero Monthly Dependencies: Built entirely with open-source technologies (Python, FastAPI, SQLite, Playwright) with zero subscription fees.
  • Consultant-Led Engagement: Structured as individual technical consultancy deliverables, enabling rapid kickoff within 60 days.
  • Enterprise Security Compliance: Application Default Credentials (ADC), private data isolation, and containerized deployment.

Operational Governance Flow 08 / 12

The 8-Stage Assessment & Publishing Lifecycle

Stage 01 · Admin Project Setup Define cycle, select OSI/LOSI, configure 102/95 indicators, and assign assessor roster
Stage 02 · System MSQ Link Ingestion Ingest 50+ page MSQs to extract declared official sub-portal URLs for link reference
Stage 03 · AI Engine AI Shallow Pre-Fill Dual agents evaluate portals with shallow citizen navigation; generate evidence with ≥75% confidence
Stage 04 · Assessors Blind A/B Intake Assessors A & B evaluate independently; submit 100% blind answers
Stage 05 · Gate Discrepancy Check Automated check: divergence >5% flags unit and triggers in-platform dual-context diff workspace
Stage 06 · Assessors Joint Reconciliation Assessors view own vs peer submissions side-by-side, resolve conflicts, and submit 1 unified answer
Stage 07 · Reviewer Senior Sign-Off Authoritative verification, dispute resolution, and publication authorization
Stage 08 · Public Instant Publish Direct projection to EGKB; live country profiles & leaderboards update instantly
Strict Governance: Human assessors are never bypassed. The AI serves as an evidence accelerator; every single published score is backed by verified human sign-off and an immutable audit log.

Discrepancy Threshold: Configurable by project (default: 5% differing answer rate). Uncontested portals pass smoothly to reviewer verification.

AI Pre-Fill Architecture 09 / 12

Inside the Decoupled AI Pre-Fill Pipeline

01. Resolve

3-Tier Link Resolution — Resolves target URLs systematically: (1) Historical KB links → (2) MSQ declared government sub-portals for reference → (3) Search engine discovery.

02. Traverse

Shallow Citizen Navigation & Crawling — Playwright limits crawl depth to citizen findability paths (first-page search discovery & top-level menus), strictly avoiding deep-link traversal to adhere to scoring rules.

03. Evaluate

Dual Independent AI Agents — Two Gemini models running on Vertex AI evaluate each indicator (102 National / 95 LOSI): Agent 1 (Literal Reader) vs Agent 2 (Inferential Context Reader) with zero cross-agent context sharing.

04. Validate

Live Citation Verification Gate — A dedicated validator model re-checks every extracted snippet and URL citation against the live rendered HTML to eradicate hallucinations.

05. Gate & Escalate

Confidence & HITL Escalation Gate — Results achieving ≥75% confidence with dual-agent agreement are presented as pre-fill suggestions. Items <75%, disputed claims, or login-gated services escalate directly to human assessors.

Key Safety Guarantee: AI suggestions are never published autonomously. Assessors can accept, modify, or completely override suggestions with 1-click simplicity.
